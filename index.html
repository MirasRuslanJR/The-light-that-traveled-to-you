    How this is calculated

    Star positions and distances come from the HYG v4.1 catalogue (mostly Hipparcos measurements). The sky is built with the formulas of Jean Meeus’s “Astronomical Algorithms”: sidereal time, precession, refraction, the Moon, the Sun and the planets. The core formulas are checked against the book’s worked examples.

    Light covers one light-year in one Julian year. The sphere on screen is the distance light has traveled since the moment you were born. Distances to nearby stars are known to a few percent, to distant ones less well, so arrival dates are given to the month. The Moon is drawn three times its real size so its phase is visible; its position (seen from where you stand) and phase are right to a fraction of a degree.
